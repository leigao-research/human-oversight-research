# Human Oversight Research

## Operational Human Oversight for Agentic and AI-Enabled Systems

This repository is a public research hub maintained by **Lei Gao**, focusing on how meaningful human intervention can be operationally embedded into increasingly autonomous and agentic AI systems.

The core research question is:

> How should human authority, intervention, escalation, override, suspension, resumption, and accountability be operationally structured across AI trust and decision lifecycles?

## 1. ITU-T FG-TIDA — Theme #16

### Operational Human Oversight Integration for Agentic AI Trust Systems

**Role:** Proposer of Theme #16 within the public working process of ITU-T FG-TIDA.

The theme explores how Human Oversight can be operationally integrated into agentic AI trust architectures and trust lifecycles.

Current areas include:

- trust events that should trigger human intervention
- escalation based on uncertainty, trust degradation, anomalous behaviour or policy conflict
- temporary suspension pending human review
- authority requirements for meaningful human intervention
- human override and continuation decisions
- return-to-operation conditions
- logging and evidence requirements for auditability and accountability

A developing operational structure links:

**trust event → intervention trigger → escalation level → human authority → permitted action → evidence requirement → return-to-operation condition**

Public working page:  
https://github.com/FG-TIDA/themes/issues/16

## 2. ITU-T FG-AI4SSC — FGAI4SSC-I-015

### Human Oversight in AI-Enabled Smart Sustainable Cities

**Role:** Author of formal input document FGAI4SSC-I-015 to the ITU-T Focus Group on AI for Smart Sustainable Cities and Communities.

This work examines how operational Human Oversight mechanisms can be applied in AI-enabled urban systems.

Key areas include:

- intervention triggers
- escalation
- qualified human review
- human override
- traceable logging
- accountable decision processes
- human control in AI-enabled city systems

This contribution provides an applied smart-city context for the broader Human Oversight research programme.
